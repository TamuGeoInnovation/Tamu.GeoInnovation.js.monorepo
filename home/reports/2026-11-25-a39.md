## What was slow, measured rather than guessed

The two greps the batch named were not most of it. **The cost was process starts.** For every heading in the file, live or not, the
loop started about eight processes: `printf`, `sed`, a `grep` of the whole file for notes, `grep` for the date, `date`, `sed` twice and
`tr`, plus a `git log -S` for each undated heading. On Windows each costs tens of milliseconds, so 364 headings is two minutes.

The heading loop now runs inside the shell, and each rule is the one it replaces:
- the id, the director word and the date are read with `[[ =~ ]]`, with the original patterns kept verbatim in variables;
- **dates** are converted to UTC midnight in shell arithmetic, and impossible dates (31 November, 29 February outside leap years, day 0)
  are rejected exactly as `date -u -d` rejected them (checked against `date` itself);
- **every undated heading is dated from one `git log -p`** over the window. Each heading's occurrences on added lines minus removed
  lines is the change in its count that `-S` looks for, and the oldest such commit is what `tail -n 1` picked;
- **withdrawn and moved notes** are found in one `awk` pass with the same regex. It reads only the lines that can match (they say
  "batch" and one of the words). The `\b[^.]*` after an id ending in a digit is spelled "not a letter, digit, `_` or full stop".

**On your caution about scoping the notes grep:** I did **not** scope it to the batch's own section. That would have dropped exactly the
case you named, "batches 12 and 13 withdrawn" written beside neither. The notes are still read across the whole file. The speed came from
doing it once rather than once per heading.

## The archive boundary, since you asked for a view

The natural boundary is the one `queued.sh` already uses: **headings outside the window (7 days by default) whose batch is reported
done, withdrawn or moved.** No tool reads them.
- `queued.sh` drops them by date.
- `board.sh` calls them "ended".

Moving them to `<name>/ARCHIVE.md` changes nothing any script prints, as long as two things move with care:
1. **Notes that withdraw or move batches.** They are read across the whole file, so a note still pointing at a live batch must stay.
2. **An undated heading's history.** It is dated from the commit that wrote it, which the move does not change, since only commits inside
   the window are read.

A batch still queued or unfinished stays wherever its age. That is the maintainer's decision, as you said. The harness here would prove
an archive changes no output.
