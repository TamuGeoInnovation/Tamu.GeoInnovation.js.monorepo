## Part 1: #3477, one reader for "latitude, longitude". Branch ready: `refactor/3477-one-coordinate-reader` (`058c86791`)

**The reader.** `parseLatLon(text): LatLon | undefined`, beside `parseLatLonLines` in `@tamu-gisc/common/utils/geometry`.
- It needs exactly two parts, separated by a comma, spaces, or both. Each part must be a whole number (`Number`, not `parseFloat`) within
  range: |lat| ≤ 90 and |lon| ≤ 180, through the existing `isLatLon`.
- `parseLatLonLines` now maps its lines through it.
- `isCoordinatePair` and `parseCoordinates` are **deleted**: no caller is left.
- **The callers:** the map search (`search.component.ts`), which no longer imports the generic library at all, and the trip planner's stop
  classification (`trip-planner.service.ts`) and its name and URL branches (`trip-planner-core.ts`).

**Order: latitude first, and a swapped pair is refused, not swapped.** Everything agrees on that order:
- the old `parseCoordinates` doc ("First number is assumed latitude");
- the changelog's `stops=@30.61438,-96.34336`;
- `trip-planner-core` writing names back as `${latitude}, ${longitude}`;
- GIS Day's lines (Google Maps' "lat, lon").

**The spec** says what the reader accepts and what it rejects:
- **Accepts:** `"30.6155, -96.3410"`, extra spaces, integers.
- **Rejects:** out of range (`"200, 500"`, `"91, 0"`, `"0, 181"`), the wrong count (`"30.6"`, `"1,2,3"`), non-numbers
  (`"30.6abc, -96.3"`, `"a, b"`), the empty string, and the swapped `"-96.3410, 30.6155"`.

**Who used which:**

  the nearest parking lot (`trip-planner.service.ts`).
- `distanceInMetres`: the trip planner's door choice and last stretch, and GIS Day's competitions leaderboard and map. Those are
  untouched.

**The change:**
- `findNearestIndex` is now `minBy` over `distanceInMetres` to each feature's centroid, and takes any geometry, not only points.
- `relativeDistance` is **deleted**: the bike and bus services call `findNearestIndex` instead of `relativeDistance` plus their own
  minimum.
- `getSmallestIndex` (`common-utils-number`) is **deleted**; `findNearestIndex` was its only caller. Its geojson-utils import is gone
  too; the package stays, used elsewhere.

**The spec records the table:**
- the six pairs above, each to 0.01 m;
- six cases asserting agreement with geojson-utils to 0.02 m;
- `findNearestIndex` cases: the nearest of three places, the two racks 3 m apart, a polygon's centroid, a tie, an empty list.
