The trip planner on dev now routes on the network Transportation published as `RoutingProPublish`, not the
old `Routing/20220119` network that no longer finds a route. The planner finds each of its travel modes on the

### GIS Day: copy a season from one GIS Day site to another

A new admin page, **Copy to another site**, exports a season from one GIS Day site as a file, and imports
that file on another. It is how UCGIS's new site is pre-loaded with Texas A&M's 2026 events, and how events
can later be handed to each university's own site.

- **What moves:** the season and its days, tags and organizations, its events, and what the events bring:
  their speakers, the speakers' affiliations and universities, the venues and broadcasts they use, and the
  logos and photos of all of those.
- **What never moves:** people's contact details (speakers' email addresses, organizations' contact
  people), a broadcast's host link and password, and anything belonging to someone's account: accounts,
  registrations, check-ins, classes, submissions and survey answers.
- **An import shows what it would do first:** how many of each kind it would add, update or find already
  the same. Nothing is written until **Apply**, and nothing is ever deleted. Records keep their identity,
  so importing again updates what is there instead of duplicating it, and fetches any logos or photos that
  are still missing.
- **It refuses** a file whose season's year this site already has as a different season, so a site never
  ends up with two of the same year.

Only GIS Day admins can export or import
([#2078](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/2078)).

### The four front ends are served by nginx 1.30 on a supported Alpine

AggieMap, GIS Day, geoservices and mailroom are served from one shared web server image, `nginx-base`. It
was built in November 2024 on nginx 1.23.2 and Alpine 3.16.3, which is no longer supported and carried 4
critical and 27 high known vulnerabilities, among them curl, OpenSSL 1.1.1, libwebp and HTTP/2 Rapid Reset.
It is now nginx 1.30.6, the current stable line, on Alpine 3.24.3, with none
([#2119](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/2119),
[nginx-base#1](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/nginx-base/pull/1)). The sites themselves are
unchanged: the same files are served, byte for byte.

The front ends now name the exact base image they are built on
([#2136](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/2136)), rather than
whatever was newest, so a change to it is always a deliberate step like this one.

### GIS Day: the 2026 VGI competition scores a memorial already documented in 2025 as 1 point

The 2026 Monument Trail Challenge gives 2 points for a new find and 1 for one already found, and "already
found" now includes the 2025 competition: every 2025 submission that was not discarded, verified or not
reviewed. 2025's statues and monuments count for monuments, its tree and bench plaques for plaques, and its
art not at all.

Plaques sit close together (half of 2025's are within 4.5 meters of another), so 15 meters, the distance
used until now, would have scored most new plaques as already found. A plaque now counts as already found
only within 10 meters of an earlier one **with the same name**, or within 3 meters of a 2025 plaque, which
has no name. Names are compared ignoring case, punctuation, a small typing mistake and a plaque's own
wording, such as "In loving memory of". Monuments and memorial markers keep 15 meters.

The rules live in the 2026 form file, beside each answer, rather than in the code, so another year can set
its own. **After release, an admin loads the 2026 form in the Designer again and saves it**, as for
[#2160](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/2160); until then,
scoring works as before. Loading a form resets every question's title and instructions to the file's, so any
wording an admin typed into the Designer since has to be typed again before saving. Releasing #2160 and this
together needs the load only once.

Whatever names they type, a participant's own second submission of the same type close to their first scores 1,
so nobody scores twice at one plaque by retyping its name. A name made only of a plaque's wording ("In loving
memory", "Dr."), or in a script other than Latin letters, counts as no name, and that plaque is judged by distance.
([#2162](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/2162))

### GIS Day: speaker photos are stored at the size the site shows them

Speaker photos were stored and served exactly as uploaded, so Presenters downloaded 60 photos, 9.0 MB, to draw cards
255 px wide, and five photos were over 500 KB. An uploaded photo is now scaled down until its shorter side is 840 px:
twice the 420 px a presenter's own page draws it at, for sharp screens. It keeps its name and aspect ratio, is
re-encoded (JPEG at quality 80; a PNG with transparency stays a PNG, and an opaque PNG is stored as whichever of JPEG and

names, so no database row changes. It is a dry run unless given `--apply`, which copies the originals aside first. The
maintainer runs it on production's uploads after the release.

### GIS Day: the competition app uploads smaller photographs, and tries again on a weak signal

The VGI competition app uploaded each photograph as the phone's camera took it, typically 2 to 8 MB,
over mobile data on campus ([#1786](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/1786)). It now shrinks each photograph on the phone first, to at
most 1,600 px on its longest side: a 12.3 MB test photograph went up as 0.67 MB. The phone decodes it
straight to that size, so a large photograph does not have to fit in its memory at full size. Every
photograph is redrawn, even one that is already small, so none carries the camera's embedded data,
such as where it was taken. An upload that does not reach the server is sent again after 2, 5 and 10
seconds before the app shows Try Again, and a second tap on Submit while it is sending no longer
submits twice. Each submission carries an id the app makes once, the same on every retry and on Try
Again, so an upload that was saved but whose answer was lost on the way back is not saved a second
time. The server refuses a photograph over 15 MB with a message saying so. Nothing on the screen looks
different.

### GIS Day: the VGI competition scores 2 for a new find and 1 for one already submitted

The VGI leaderboard gave every submission 1 point, or the points of its type answer, so ten people
photographing one monument scored ten times what one did ([#1781](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/1781)). Now the first submission
of a thing scores **2 points**, and every later submission of it, by anyone, the first submitter
included, scores **1 point**. A submission is of something already submitted when it is within 15 m
of an earlier new find with the same type; the distance, the same every season, allows for phone GPS
error. Only new finds are compared with, so submissions a few metres apart in a line do not chain
into one thing. Two submissions made in the same instant are always taken in the same order.
Discarded submissions do not count, so if the first one is discarded the next becomes the new find.
The type question's per-answer points are no longer used: the form designer no longer asks for them,
and the admin and participant submission lists no longer show a Points column that disagreed with the
leaderboard. The leaderboard for a past season, by its guid, is scored the same way. The 2026
competition page says how points work now.

### GIS Day: check-in to an in-person session needs the attendee to be there

Anyone could check in to a session from anywhere, so a check-in proved nothing about attending. Now an
admin can give each venue a **check-in zone**: its corners, typed on the venue form one
`latitude, longitude` per line, as Google Maps copies them. For an **in-person** session at a venue with
a zone, pressing Check In asks the browser for the attendee's location, and the page says so beside the
button ("Check-in for this session uses your location: you need to be at TAMU Rudder Plaza.").

- **Inside the zone** the attendee checks in as before. A phone reports how accurate its location is;
  an attendee counts as inside when that circle reaches the zone, with the accuracy counted up to
  100 m, so someone at a window of a large building gets in and a vague position from across town
  does not.
- **Outside it**, check-in is refused, and the reason shows under the button: "You need to be inside
  the check-in zone at TAMU Rudder Plaza to check in to this event." The API makes the decision, so
  calling it directly does not get round it. There is no checking in anyway: an organizer adds an
  exception from Admin, Check-ins, Add ([#1767](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/1767)), which checks in the attendee named, asks for no
  location and is not held to the zone.
- **While the browser looks for the location** the page says "Finding your location…" and the button
  waits. If location access is blocked, the page asks for it; if finding the location takes too long,
  it says so and suggests trying near a window.
- **Hybrid and virtual sessions**, and venues with no zone (such as the bar for Mappy Hour), check in as
  before. Nothing about where an attendee was is saved.
- **A zone typed wrongly is refused**: fewer than three corners, a line that is not a latitude and
  longitude, or corners out of order so its sides cross.

A determined person can still make their browser report a made-up location; the server cannot tell.
This stops checking in from home, not deliberate spoofing.

It needs a database migration, `AddCheckInZones`, run on production before this release; the plan is on
[#1766](https://github.com/TamuGeoInnovation/Tamu.GeoInnovation.js.monorepo/issues/1766). Until a venue is given a zone, its sessions check in exactly as today. The 2026 zones
for the Memorial Student Center, Rudder Plaza and the OMB lobby are on the issue.

The images are a local copy, taken before the review changes: the pop-up offering to check in anyway
is gone, and new images of the refusal, waiting and timeout messages are still to be taken.
