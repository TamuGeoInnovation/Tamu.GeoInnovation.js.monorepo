## Flakiness, which is the whole difficulty

1. **No retries for these tests** (`test.describe.configure({ retries: 0 })`), because the suite's `retries: 2` turns a one-in-four fault into a pass nearly every time.
2. **One load per test**, so a failure says "load 2 of 6 was blank" and the report shows how many of six; a fault that blanks every load fails all six.
3. **The test itself had to stop being flaky first.** The first version ran the six loads on the suite's six workers with a 45 s allowance and failed 1 to 4 of 6 on healthy maps: under six-way contention a drawn map took 35 to 55 s (`share` 0.954, then 0.264). That was slowness, not the bug. The fix: tag them `@heavy` (two at a time, the project's own mechanism for exactly this) and allow 60 s. A blank map stays white (0.952) for the whole allowance; a healthy one draws in about 20 to 35 s here with two workers.
4. A remaining risk: a very slow machine could still push a healthy draw past 60 s. The failure message says what it measured (share and colour), so a slow draw (below 0.65 at the end) reads differently from a blank (0.952).

## What it costs

On the release tier it adds **about 100 to 115 s of wall clock when it passes** (six tests, two at a time, about 20 to 35 s each including the 5 s hold). When the map is blank, each blank load holds a worker 60 s: **about 200 s** for all six. The heavy project runs after everything else, so it extends the run by that much unless other heavy tests are still running beside it.
