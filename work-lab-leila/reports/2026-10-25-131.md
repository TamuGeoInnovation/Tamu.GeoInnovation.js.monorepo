## The bound chosen

**8000 px a side, or 40 megapixels in all**, as asked. `IMAGE_SIZE_LIMITS` in `libs/common/node/images/src/lib/image-size.ts` says why beside
it:
- 40 MP decodes to 160 MB, more than any camera photo a person would upload, and well within the API's memory;
- a 6000 × 4000 (24 MP) photo is allowed;
- 8001 a side, or 7000 × 7000 (49 MP), is refused.

## What it does

- **`readImageSize(buffer)`** reads the declared width and height **from the header alone**, with no decoding and no new dependency:
  - PNG: `IHDR`;
  - JPEG: walks the segments to the first start-of-frame (`SOF0` to `SOF15`, skipping `DHT`, `JPG` and `DAC`);
  - GIF: the logical screen;
  - WebP: `VP8 `, `VP8L` and `VP8X`.

  It returns `null` for anything else, or for a header that is too short or reads as 0. It lives in `common-node-images`, beside `fitImage`.
- **The speaker provider refuses** before anything decodes or stores the photo:
  - **over the bound: 413** (`PayloadTooLargeException`): *The photo is too large in pixels: 50000 x 50000. The most is 8000 a side and 40
    megapixels.*
  - **an unreadable header: 400** (`BadRequestException`): *The photo must be a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP image.* This includes SVG, which can carry
    script.
- **On create, the check runs before the speaker is saved,** so a refused photo leaves no speaker behind. Without that, `insertWithInfo`
  saved the speaker first and failed on the photo after.
- **`fitImage` itself never decodes an image over the bound.** It returns the file unchanged, *too large in pixels to decode*. So
  `fit-speaker-photos` is safe on files stored before this check existed.
- **The crafted files are shared fixtures** (`declaredPng`, `declaredJpeg`, exported from the library), not one-off helpers in each spec.
