## 2. What is failing

| | Layer 1, Structure Data | Layer 2, University Building | Layer 9, Surface Parking |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Queries | 960 | 960 | 960 |
| `500 Error performing query operation` | 14 | 12 | 13 |
| `400 Invalid or missing input parameters.` | **27** | **34** | **0** |
| Failed | 41 (4.27%) | 46 (4.79%) | 13 (1.35%) |

- **Every error arrives as HTTP 200,** with the error in the body, as #2702 says. There were no network errors and no
  HTTP errors, and none of the 240 non-query requests (the service and layer descriptions) failed.
- **One kind of request:** every query is a tile of features (`f=pbf`, quantized, `resultType=tile`,
  `orderByFields=OBJECTID ASC`), the same for all three layers except `outFields` and `where` (layer 2 asks for
  `OBJECTID` only, where `Type = 'CONSTRUCTION'`).
- **The 400 isn't about the request.** It says the parameters are invalid, but **all 61 URLs that got it succeeded at
  another moment, unchanged.** So the parameters are fine and the server is wrong. It's likely the same family as the
  `Invalid or missing input parameters.` I saw intermittently on 22 October on ordered queries (report 2026-10-22-2).
- **Not the fields.** Layer 2's queries ask only for `OBJECTID` and still get it.
- **Not a hot spot.** The 400s fall across all 12 tiles (1 to 6 of 80 each, no outlier). Per load, 0, 1 or 2 of them,
  close to what chance gives.
- **What layers 1 and 2 share, and layer 9 doesn't:** both are the building polygons, with the same 27 fields (1,003 and
  1,802 features), while layer 9 has 12 fields and 341 features. All three are polygons in the same source spatial
  reference (32139), with no definition expression and no relationships. **I can't see from outside why only the
  buildings get the 400.** It's the GIS team's to find, and "building layers 1 and 2, since overnight on 8 to 9 November"
  is the lead to give them.
- **Not concurrency, and not the browser.** I replayed one load's 36 queries from Node, 10 rounds asked one at a time and
  10 rounds asked all 36 together. **One at a time: 15 of 360 failed (4.17%). All together: 11 of 360 (3.06%).** Both
  kinds of error appear either way. So the server fails a flat share of requests, however they arrive.
- **Answer times are normal:** median 304 ms for good answers, 312 ms for failures. Failures come back as fast as
  successes, so it isn't a timeout.

**One fault or two?** Two, with different signatures: the old 500 on every layer at an unchanged rate, and a new 400 on
two layers since overnight. But they're the same service, the same symptom for a visitor (a tile that doesn't draw) and
the same owner. So **I'd report both on #2702 rather than file a new issue**, with the 400 named as new so the GIS team
doesn't read it as the old one getting worse. If they find the 400 has a separate cause, that's when it gets its own
issue.

## 3. The threshold

**It caught this, but only by luck.** The exact chance a 10-load run (360 queries) fails the check:

| Limit, queries per run | at 1.5% | at 2.5% | at 3.6% (today) | at 5% | at 8% |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **4% of 360 (today's check)** | 1 in 2,271 | 3.9% | **31.9%** | 79.9% | 99.9% |
| 3% of 360 | 2.2% | 29.2% | 75.0% | 97.3% | 100% |
| 2.5% of 360 | 4.7% | 41.3% | 83.6% | 98.6% | 100% |
| 4% of 720 | 1 in 377,861 | 0.9% | 29.5% | 90.4% | 100% |
| **3% of 720 (20 loads)** | **0.2%** | 19.8% | **81.0%** | 99.6% | 100% |
| 2.5% of 720 | 1.4% | 43.8% | 93.7% | 99.9% | 100% |

**At today's rate, the current check fails about one run in three.** Batch 23's two runs both failing was a 1-in-10 draw.
So the check will flap on today's service, and a red run will read as noise the next day. You're right that 4% against a
1.5% baseline let a doubling pass.

**My recommendation: 20 loads and a 3% limit.** I haven't changed anything.
- **Quiet:** at the old 1.5%, it fails about 1 run in 500. On two nightly runs a day, that's a false alarm about every
  eight months.
- **Catches today's rate:** at 3.6%, it fails 4 runs in 5, and at 5% it fails almost always.
- **The cost:** the test takes about 3 minutes instead of 1.5. Both knobs exist already (`AGGIEMAP_SMOKE_BASEMAP_LOADS`,
  `AGGIEMAP_SMOKE_BASEMAP_MAX_FAILED_RATE`), so it's two defaults and the comment's numbers.
- **Its consequence:** on today's service it fails nearly every night until the GIS team fixes the 400. That's right, as
  the service is degraded, but it'll redden the nightly. The alternative is a known-failure entry tied to #2702.
  Deciding that is yours.

**A second, cheaper improvement:** the check's log line could count failures by error message, e.g.
`61 × 400 Invalid or missing input parameters., 39 × 500 Error performing query operation`. A new kind of error would
then show up on the first run, whatever the rate. That's how this morning's change was found here, and the check
couldn't have shown it.

**Something worth knowing for the fix:** 99 of 100 failed queries succeeded on the first re-ask. If the map asked once
more on an error body, a visitor's loss would drop from about 3.5% of tiles to about 0.1%. That's an application change
(an Esri request interceptor for the basemap's queries), not a test change, and not asked for. I mention it because it
would end the visible fault whatever the service does.
