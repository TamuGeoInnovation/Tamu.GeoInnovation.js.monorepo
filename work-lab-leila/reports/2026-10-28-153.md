## Task 1: `layer-order.spec.ts` on Indoor Track

### What it asserted, and what the map does

**What it asserted:** `indoor-track-parking-team-bus` draws above `indoor-track-parking-building` (#2059), read from the probe's layer
order on three fresh loads.

**What exists now**, read on production and on dev:
- **The republished service, `IndoorTrackParking_view`, has three layers:** `0` Accessible Parking Spaces (2 points), `1` Indoor
  Track Building (1 polygon), `2` Indoor Track Event Parking Lots (5 polygons). There's no team bus area in it.
- **The definition:** #2468 removed `TEAM_BUS_PARKING`, along with Visitor Kiosk and Safety First. Both environments read the one
  schema's sources (`/0`, `/1`, `/2`), and all three layers load and are counted.
- **The map's order**, bottom first, the same on both environments: building, then lots, then accessible spaces. That's the
  definition's declared order. `EventService` adds the layers in the reverse of the `INDOOR_TRACK_PARKING_LAYERS` enum's order.

So the test was waiting for a layer the map no longer has, and timed out on every run.

**Which order matters now.** I ran a spatial query on production's service:
- **Both accessible spaces lie inside lots:** one in Lot 100j, one in Lot 100g.
- **No lot touches the building.**

So the order a regression could break is **accessible spaces above lots**: the other way round, the lots' fill covers the wheelchair
symbols, with every layer still loaded and counted. Nothing else in the suite would notice. That's the same kind of fault #2059 was
about.
