## What I built, and the two choices

**One group, not three layers.** **Micromobility**, listed as one entry and off until a visitor turns it on. It holds the
service's three layers in the service's order: Corridor (12 features), Allowed Sidewalks (28) and Facilities (255).
- **Why:** they're one subject, the main map's list already has eleven entries, and Sustainable Transportation already
  groups the bike layers the same way (`listMode: 'hide-children'`).
- **Turned on,** all three draw, and the legend lists them separately with the service's own symbols.

**Names and symbols from the service.** No layer has a `title` or a renderer, so each takes the service's name through
the existing untitled-layer rule (#1802), and no `layer-title-overrides.json` entry is needed. Only the group is titled,
because no service names a group.

**The popup:** each line shows its `name` and its `notes` as its type, as the Micromobility map does (for example
"Restricted Sharrow / Type: Roadway"). The `notes` field is what the service's own renderer keys Facilities on.

**The gate: `DEVELOPMENT_ONLY_LAYER_SOURCES`, not `TestingService`.** The batch says "`TestingService` / `isTesting`", but
for a main-map layer the repository's mechanism is the #2673 list, which Nature Rx uses: **a production build leaves the
group out altogether**. That's stronger than a runtime check, and it's what the list was made for (its comment cites
#1090, a template gate nothing asserted). So `MICROMOBILITY` joins that list, with `#2739` as its reason.

**The smoke entry: `/map` added, after checking it's right.** `development-only.spec.ts` requires dev to request a
service from every page its entry lists, and an off-by-default layer might never be requested.
- **What I measured:** the main map **asks for the three layers' descriptions as it loads, group off or not**: `/0`,
  `/1` and `/2` on every load of `/map`. So `/map` is right in the list. Dev requests the service there unprompted, and
  production is checked never to.
- **The same is true of Nature Rx** (below), whose entry lists no pages on the opposite assumption.

## The overlap with the bike layers (input for the maintainer's decision; nothing changed)

**How I measured it:**
- Each micromobility line was sampled every 5 m. A sample counts as overlapping when it's within 8 m of a bike layer's
  line.
- The bike layers were read from production's `TS/BikeMap` (they're dead on dev, #2744).
- Campus Bike Lanes has 3,186 features over a 2,000-feature page, so I paged it: my first pass had missed a third of it.

| Micromobility layer | Length | Along Campus Bike Lanes | Along City Bike Lanes and Routes | Along any |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Micromobility Facilities (255) | 34.7 km | **66%** | 0% | 67% |
| Allowed Sidewalks (28) | 18.4 km | 11% | 11% | 18% |
| Micromobility Corridor (12) | 1.9 km | 1% | 0% | 1% |

**What it says:**
- **Facilities largely redraws the campus bike lanes:** two-thirds of it runs along them, and the other third is new.
- **Allowed Sidewalks and the Corridor are mostly new information,** where a scooter may ride on a sidewalk and the slow
  zones, which no bike layer has.
- **So the Corridor and Allowed Sidewalks don't replace anything.** If something were to go, it would be Campus Bike
  Lanes in favour of Facilities, not the reverse. Facilities covers about 23 km of its lines and Campus Bike Lanes has
  more besides, so that wants a look at what the remaining third of Campus Bike Lanes is.
- **Not measured:**
  - Dismount Zones are areas, not lines, so they aren't in the table.
  - Bike Racks and Fix Stations are points, with nothing to overlap.
