## The answer

**`setSpriteSource` with an image sheet blanks the map when it lands while the view is building or drawing the layer. Called before
the layer is in any map, it never blanks.**

| When the sprite is set | Blank loads |
| --- | --- |
| after `view.whenLayerView()` (#2481) | 15 of 15 (report 148) |
| on `layer.when()`, while the layer goes into the map, as today | **9 of 60 tonight**; 4 of 15 in report 148 |
| never | 0 of 15 (report 148) |
| **after `await layer.load()`, before `new Map(...)`, so before any view or layer view** | **0 of 45** |

- **The hatch draws** in the last row: 1524 is the orange grid, grass between the lines, with an unbroken black outline and the
  labels on top (`e2-aplin.png`, `zoom-e2-aplin.png`).
- **The ordering 10:25 PM's note suggested works.** At the control's rate tonight, 0 of 45 would happen by chance about 0.07% of the
  time.

## The experiments

All are on `development` (`810261679`), measured as in report 148: 15 plain loads of `/map/d` per block, each on a fresh dev server
from a clean worktree, without `--poll`, with blank meaning pure white for 30 s. The control blocks were interleaved in the same
session (report 151).

### 1. Our image, or the call?

| Variant | What `applySpriteOverrides` did | Blank |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **e1a** | the **published sheet, unmodified**: the published image drawn on a canvas, with the published index, and no hatch appended or drawn. Then `setSpriteSource({ type: 'image', ... })`, as today, on `layer.when()` | **1 of 15** |
| **e1b** | `setSpriteSource({ type: 'url', spriteUrl })` with the layer's **own published sprite URL**, on `layer.when()` | **0 of 15** |
| control | today's hatch, `type: 'image'`, on `layer.when()` | 9 of 60 |

- **e1a blanked with no change to the image at all.** So the content of our sheet isn't what blanks the map: **the `image` call
  does**, wherever it lands in the layer's life.
- **e1b, with the `url` form, didn't blank** in 15. That's too few to call it safe, but it's consistent: that form hands Esri a URL to
  load as it would its own, where `image` swaps a decoded sheet into a layer that may already be drawing.
- **e1a's rate, 1 of 15, is lower than the control's**, but that's not a significant difference at 15 loads.

### 2. What the renderer does

On every blank load, as in report 148, the probe reports the view ready, spatial reference 3857, and every layer loaded and
drawable, with `drawing` false. So **the view thinks it has finished**, there's no error to see, and it never paints. It never
recovers, either: a blank load stays white for the full 30 s, and for 75 s in report 148.

That points at the renderer:
- the sprite change arrives while the layer view is building its first tiles;
- the view ends up with tiles it believes are drawn and a canvas with nothing on it;
- nothing makes it redraw.

**I didn't capture WebGL errors or Esri's debug log on a blank load.** The console showed only the usual layer-load failures for
services on gis-dev. Proving exactly which internal step it is would need Esri's debug build. I don't think that's needed for the
decision below.

### 3. Is it a race?

**Yes.** The evidence:
- the later the call, the worse: after the layer view 15 of 15, during creation about 1 in 7, before the map 0 of 45;
- the same build blanks some loads and not others, at a rate that varies by machine: 9 of 60 here, 1 of 18 for copilot-home, 0 for
  home's control;
- the unmodified sheet still blanks (e1a).

**What it races:** the sheet being swapped (`setSpriteSource` with an image) against the layer view's first draw. If the swap lands
before the layer view exists, there's nothing to race. That's experiment 4.

### 4. The ordering nobody had tried: load, set, then the map (e2)

The change (experiment worktree `C:/TAMU/wt-2487-e2`; about 20 lines, not pushed):
- **`makeBaseLayer`** no longer applies the overrides on `layer.when()`. It records them against the layer.
- **`EsriMapService.makeMap`**, which is already `async`, then works through the basemap's base layers before `new Map(basemap)`.
  For each one with overrides, it runs `await layer.load()`, then `await applySpriteOverrides(...)`, the existing function,
  unchanged.
- **Failures are caught and logged**, so a broken override still can't stop the map.

| Block (in order) | Blank |
| --- | --- |
| first, after the other experiments | 0 of 15 |
| second, between two control blocks | 0 of 15 |
| third | 0 of 15 |
| **e2, total** | **0 of 45**, against the control's 9 of 60 in the same session |

**Why I believe it, not just the number:**
- it removes the race itself. The layer view is created from a style whose sprite is already the patched one, so it never has to
  take a sheet swap;
- it agrees with every other row: the closer the call is to an existing renderer, the more often the map blanks.

**What it costs and doesn't cover:**
- **A slower first draw.** The map now waits for the style and sprite to load, and the patch, before it's built. That's one fetch
  of the style and one of the sheet, about the same time the basemap took to draw before anyway. I didn't measure the delay
  separately.
- **Only the first basemap.** Switching basemaps in the gallery builds its layers elsewhere (`basemap-gallery.service.ts`, #2477's
  file). That path would need the same treatment: build the layer, `load()`, apply, and only then hand it to the view. Until then a
  gallery switch to the vector basemap would race again, or, as today, draw the published pink pattern, which is what it does now.
- **Reloading the style.** Anything that reloads the style later (`loadStyle`) would also put back the published sprite.

### 5. `loadStyle` with a replacement style

**Not tried.** Experiment 4 answered the question first, and `loadStyle` replaces the whole style while the layer view exists, which
is the condition experiments 1 to 3 implicate. I'd expect it to share the fault. It could be tested the same way if anyone wants the
row filled in.

## So: three ways to get the hatch

| Approach | Blank loads | 1524 | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| today (`image`, on `layer.when()`) | 9 of 60 | orange hatch | the fault |
| **load, set, then the map (e2)** | **0 of 45** | orange hatch, under the outline and labels | keeps the sprite code; the gallery path needs the same order |
| **home's hatch layer** (`feat/2459-hatch-layer`, report 151) | **0 of 45** | orange grid, **over** the outline, labels and trees | deletes the sprite code (-724 lines); independent of Esri's sprite internals |

**Both are clean on this rig.** The choice is the maintainer's:
- **e2** keeps today's look exactly, and relies on the order staying right;
- **home's layer** removes the risky API altogether, at the cost of drawing over the outline and labels unless it's moved below
  them.

## Questions for the director (aggiemap)
