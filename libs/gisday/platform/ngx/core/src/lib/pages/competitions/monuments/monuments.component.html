Texas A&M University Monument Trail Challenge

TxGIS Day, 2026

The TxGIS Day Organizing Committee is excited to invite participants to participate in this year's VGI competition by documenting the monuments and memorial plaques on Texas A&M's campus!

Monuments and campus memorials are an essential part of creating a compelling atmosphere at Texas A&M and across universities worldwide, providing opportunities for thoughtful reflection and enriching the spirit and culture of the University.

The challenge aims to survey and document monuments and memorial plaques across campus to help:

  • Raise awareness: Identifying and documenting campus monuments and memorials gives participants an opportunity to think about the environment that surrounds them daily.
  • Provide Reflection: Monuments and memorials are designed to evoke and communicate emotion to the viewer, so inviting participants to seek these out helps to develop deeper emotional connections to Texas A&M's campus environment.
  • Protection and Preservation: documenting the locations and information of all monuments and memorials on campus helps stewards maintain these important pieces.

This event is proudly sponsored by:

From Saturday, November 14 through the week of Texas A&M GIS Day (November 16-20), participants can use their mobile devices to survey all points of Texas A&M's campus in search of:

  • Monuments
  • Memorial markers
  • Memorial plaques on trees and benches

How to participate

  • Complete the pre-competition survey before submitting any data for the competition.
  • Download and install the Official TxGIS Day Competitions App on your mobile device.
  • Ensure Location Services are ON in your phone’s camera settings so each photo records GPS coordinates. See iOS instructions and Android instructions for help.
  • Walk around campus and find a monument, a memorial marker, or a memorial tree or bench
  • At each location, open the competitions app and allow it to automatically use your GPS location.
  • Fill out the data collection form: choose what you found, and type the name on the plaque or marker, or the monument's name.
  • Take a clear photo that shows the plaque or marker and its inscription, or the monument and any plaque on it, so the name can be read.
  • Each monument or memorial can only be documented once per participant, so make sure to mark locations accurately.
  • Submit the data collection form in the app and move to the next one. You can survey as many monuments and memorials on campus as you would like.
  • Complete the post-competition survey after you have submitted all of the data you want to collect.
* Note: the competition app may be unavailable until the day of the competition.

Examples of Submissions

Below is what is being surveyed, with examples. Statues, sculptures and other art, which were part of the 2025 competition, are not included this year.

Monuments

Monuments are structures built to remember a person, a group or an event, often with an inscription or a plaque of their own.

 Memorial markers

Memorial markers are smaller memorials, such as a stone or a plaque on a post or a wall, that mark a person or an event in a particular place.

 Memorial plaques on trees and benches

These are memorial plaques associated with special trees and benches located across the university campus.

How Competition Points Work

  • 2 points for a complete submission of a monument, a memorial marker, or a bench or tree with a memorial plaque that nobody has submitted yet, this year or in the 2025 competition.
  • 1 point for a complete submission of one that has already been submitted this year, by anyone, including you, or that was documented in 2025.

A submission counts as already submitted when it is:

  • A memorial plaque within 10 meters of an earlier 2-point plaque with the same name, or within 3 meters of a plaque documented in 2025. Plaques sit close together, so the name is what tells them apart.
  • A monument within 15 meters of an earlier 2-point monument, or of a monument documented in 2025.
  • A memorial marker within 15 meters of an earlier 2-point marker.
Top 3 participants with the highest number of points will receive associated prizes.

Eligibility

While we encourage all to download the app and submit data, only graduate and undergraduate students at Texas A&M are eligible to compete for prizes.

In order to be eligible for rewards, participants must:

  • Be a current Texas A&M student.
  • Have an active TxGIS Day account with completed profile (including UIN and email).
  • Complete the pre-competition survey.
  • Complete the post-competition survey.

Rules, Participation, and Winners

To be eligible to win, participants must be a current Texas A&M student.

Participants must have an active TxGIS Day account. Using the official TxGIS Day Competitions App, users will complete surveys on monuments, memorial markers and memorial plaques anywhere on Texas A&M property in Brazos County, Texas. This includes, but is not limited to, the Main and West Campus, and the RELLIS Campus. Trespassing on secure or unpermitted areas is prohibited.

Participants must complete two brief questionnaires regarding their experience with the survey and perspective on the monuments and memorials surveyed. Submissions without these questionnaires completed will not be considered for an award.

Only submissions made between Saturday, November 14 and 11:00 AM Central on Friday, November 20 will be considered for an award. The participant with the highest number of points from verified submissions at the conclusion of the TxGIS Day event will be awarded 1st place. The next highest number will be awarded 2nd place, and the next 3rd place.

Submissions count until 11:00 AM Central on Friday, November 20.

The final winners will be announced at the lunch session on Friday, November 20.

Prizes are provided courtesy of the Texas A&M Office of the University Architect.

Participants do not need to be present at the Awards Ceremony to be selected as a winner.

Project Information Sheet

Why is this study being done?

The purpose of this study is to leverage the data collection abilities of the 2,500+ TAMU faculty, students, staff, and visitors who participate in the TxGIS Day events per year to gather data about the TAMU campus. Each year, the TxGIS Day planning committee organizes a GIS Mapping Competition where TxGIS Day participants gather data (GPS, photos, and other attributes) of amenities and services on campus. You are invited to participate in these data collection efforts!

Why am I being asked to be in the study?

You are being asked to be in this study because you have a relationship with Texas A&M either as a faculty or staff member, a student, or a visitor.

How many people will be asked to be in the study?

The study organizers hope to engage 2,500 study participants per year for each of the four years of the project. We will contact 50,000 people per year (roughly the number all TAMU students, faculty, and staff) in order to achieve these participant numbers for each year of the project.

What are the alternatives to being in this study?

The alternative to being in the study is not to participate. Participation in this study is not mandatory, and non-participation will not impact you in any fashion.

What will I be asked to do in this study?

You will be asked to walk around the TAMU campus and take pictures of monuments and campus memorial installations. In 2026, you are being asked to take pictures and document information about monuments and memorial plaques on the TAMU campus. In future years, the organizing committee will survey different aspects of the TAMU campus.

Are there are risks to me?

The things that you will be doing will pose no greater risks than the you would come across in everyday life. While collecting data, you will be submitting your location (actually the location of the thing you are collecting about), which could expose the locations that you collected data at, should these data be stolen. Other risks include a breach of confidentiality should study information about you (name, email, TAMU department, and TAMU classification) be stolen.

Will there be any costs to me?

Aside from your time, there are no costs for taking part in the study. Your participation can take any amount of time up to seven days (the full length of the week around TxGIS Day), but we expect that most people will participate for less than two hours.

Will photos, video, or audio recordings be made of me during this study?

Photos, video, and audio recordings will not be taken of you as part of this study.

Will I be paid to be in the study?

You will not be paid for being in this study, but there will be an award given to at least one of the participants who will be selected at random from all participants who create accounts. Creating an account is not mandatory. You can participate anonymously without creating an account, but in this case you will not be eligible for winning prizes (since we won't know how you are!!).

Will information from this study be kept private?

The records of this study will be kept private. Any personally identifiable information you submit during the course of creating an account will be removed from the data when it is used in publications or presentations. You may choose to submit data anonymously, in which case no identifiable information will be collected from or about you.

Research records will be stored securely in the TAMU West Campus Data Center, and only Dr. Daniel Goldberg, other members of the project team, and TAMU IT data center staff will have access to the records. Representatives of regulatory agencies such as the Office of Human Research Protections (OHRP) and entities such as the Texas A&M University Human Subjects Protection Program may access your records to make sure the study is being run correctly and that information is collected properly.

What if I change my mind about participating?

This research is voluntary and you have the choice whether or not to be in this research study. You may decide to not begin or to stop participating at any time. If you choose not to be in this study or stop being in the study, there will be no effect on your status, grades, employment, or relationship with Texas A&M University, etc. Any new information discovered about the research will be provided to you. This information could affect your willingness to continue your participation.

Whom may I contact for more information?

You may contact the Principal Investigator, Daniel Goldberg, PhD, to tell him about a concern or complaint about this research at 979-845-6395 or dan@tamu.edu.

For questions about your rights as a research participant; or if you have questions, complaints, or concerns about the research, you may call the Texas A&M University Human Subjects Protection Program office at (979) 458-4067 or irb@tamu.edu.
